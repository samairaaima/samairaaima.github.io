Starbucks vs. Luckin Coffee:
Brand Meaning in China

Brand Strategy Consumer Behaviour Case Analysis

my take

The assignment: a take-home case analysis of Starbucks and Luckin Coffee competing in China. Seven questions, one framework after another.

It was a grind. It was also the assignment where brand strategy stopped feeling theoretical to me.

The interesting problem isn't who's winning. It's why. Starbucks built something people feel: a third place, a status signal, a Saturday morning ritual. Luckin built something people use. Both are successful. Only one of them is a brand in the way that actually matters, and the data makes that uncomfortably clear when you run them through the Brand Resonance Pyramid side by side.

The part I kept coming back to was Q7: the reflection section. We'd studied the Porsche SUV case in class around the same time, and I couldn't stop drawing the parallel. The moment a brand chases convenience over identity, it risks becoming the thing it was never supposed to be. Starbucks isn't immune to that. Neither is anyone else.

What I learned that stayed: functional loyalty and emotional loyalty look identical in the short term. They don't in the long term. Luckin proves it.

Brand Resonance Pyramid applied to both brands side by side. Starbucks scores high on feelings and resonance. Luckin scores high on salience and relevance. The gap between those two profiles is the whole argument.

key finding

Luckin sold 90 million cups across 2,370 stores in its first two years. Starbucks took a decade to reach the same scale. By every functional metric, Luckin won. By every brand metric, Starbucks still leads. Scale and brand equity are not the same thing and they don't move together.
