I grew up in India, but learned a lot of what I know from the globe: 21 countries, countless airports, and an early understanding that people everywhere want similar things, but respond to very different stories.

I'm a third-year Marketing and Business Analytics student at UBC Sauder. My experience spans across digital marketing, branding, content creation, and community engagement through internships and student organizations. Every role has taught me the same thing: people don't stop for information. They stop for something that feels true.

Outside of marketing, I'm a national-level soccer player, a trained Kathak dancer with a six-year certification, and someone who has probably built half her personality around coffee. None of these sit neatly on a resume under "marketing experience," but they shape how I think: strategy, discipline, and expressing yourself.

Inside Out.

Liquid I.V. × Laneige

You replenish your body. You forget your skin.

Inside Out puts them there.

Liquid I.V. owns the inside. Laneige owns the outside. Both brands are already talking to the same person, they just haven't been in the same room yet.

This isn't about aesthetics matching. It's about a gap that actually exists: the moment your body is being replenished and your skin is being forgotten. Neither brand has closed that gap alone. Together, they can.

The Campaign

A four-part system built around movement: before, during, after, and overnight. Liquid I.V. gives back what sweat takes. Laneige supports what sweat does to the skin. One routine. Two brands. Nothing left out.

Campaign Breakdown

Spotify Wrapped: the smartest gift Spotify ever gave you

Brand campaign analysis · Spotify

Concept mockup · not affiliated with Spotify
My take

I have never had a Spotify account, but every November, Spotify Wrapped still gets my attention. That alone says something.

Every November, the internet collectively loses its mind over a feature that has been doing the same thing for years. And yet somehow, it works every single time. Spotify takes each user's listening data and turns it into a personalized recap of their year. It sounds simple because it is. That is exactly the point.

What Spotify did was take data it already had and give it back to the user as a gift.

They turned cold analytics into something emotional. Your music taste feels tied to your identity, so seeing it reflected back does not feel like a data report. It feels like being seen.

And then something even more powerful happens: people share it. Every person who uploads their Wrapped is doing Spotify's marketing for them, completely voluntarily, to their entire following.

I do not even use Spotify. I am an Apple Music person. And still, every year when Wrapped season hits, I find myself paying attention. That is how strong the concept is. It transcends the product itself.

The best marketing does not feel like marketing at all. It feels like a gift.
