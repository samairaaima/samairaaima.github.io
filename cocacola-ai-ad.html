Coca-Cola's AI Christmas Ad: When the Magic Goes Missing

Brand campaign analysis — Coca-Cola, 2024

My take

Coca-Cola's Christmas ads have always felt like the start of the holiday season. But when the brand used AI to recreate that magic, something felt off. This piece looks at why the backlash was not really about the technology itself, but about what gets lost when brands try to automate emotion.

The breakdown

Every year, there are a few signs that the holidays have officially arrived. The lights go up, the music starts playing, and Coca-Cola brings back its Christmas campaign.

For decades, those ads have been one of the most recognizable traditions in advertising. The red trucks lit up with fairy lights, the warmth, the nostalgia, Santa holding a Coke. It was never just an ad. It was a feeling. A signal that the season had begun.

So when Coca-Cola released an AI-generated version of that same campaign in 2024, the internet noticed immediately.

And not in a good way.

The ad was produced using AI studios and generative video models, creating a montage of trucks, smiling faces, and festive streets. At first glance, it looked familiar. But then the details started to feel off. Wheels that did not spin. Strange proportions. A Santa whose face never quite appeared on screen. Small things, but enough to break the illusion.

The ad no longer felt warm or nostalgic. It felt manufactured.

The technical flaws were not really the problem, though.

The bigger issue was what happened the moment audiences realized they were watching AI. Something shifted. The ad no longer felt warm or nostalgic. It felt manufactured. And when you feel cheated of an authentic human moment, no amount of fairy lights can fix that.

Coca-Cola's Christmas campaign has always worked because it felt like it was made by people who understood what the holidays feel like. The AI version felt like a copy of a feeling, not the feeling itself. And that is a significant problem for a brand built so heavily on emotion.

Coca-Cola did not need to reinvent its Christmas ad. It needed to deliver the warmth people were already expecting. AI can recreate the visual language of Christmas: the lights, the trucks, the snow, the smiles. But it struggles to create the emotional weight behind them.

The lesson is not that AI has no place in marketing. It absolutely does.

Some brand moments are too emotionally valuable to cheapen.

But some brand moments are too emotionally valuable to cheapen. Coca-Cola's Christmas campaign is one of them. The magic was never just in the trucks or the lights. It was in the human hands that made them feel magical.
