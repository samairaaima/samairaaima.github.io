Nespresso: How to Stay Relevant Without Starting Over

Brand campaign analysis: Nespresso

My take

My entire family owns a Nespresso machine. My parents, my brother, and me. Different ages, different coffee orders, same brand. I never thought much about why until I started paying attention to how Nespresso markets itself. This piece is about how they managed to stay relevant across generations without losing what made them iconic in the first place.

The breakdown

Most brands, when they feel themselves getting old, do one of two things. They panic and change everything. Or they hold on too tight and become a relic.

Nespresso did neither.

For years, Nespresso's identity was essentially George Clooney. Charming, polished, effortlessly sophisticated. The "What else?" campaign turned a tagline into a brand personality, and it worked because Clooney did not just endorse the product. He became part of what Nespresso meant. Premium coffee. A certain kind of taste. A lifestyle, not just a machine.

But audiences change. And a brand built entirely around one face and one idea of luxury is a risk.

So Nespresso did something most brands are too afraid to do. They did not replace what was working. They added to it.

They did not replace what was working. They added to it.

Enter Dua Lipa. The Vertuo World campaign pairs her with Clooney, introducing a younger, more colorful, more playful version of Nespresso. Iced coffees, bigger cups, more moods, more occasions. Where Clooney represents the classic espresso ritual, Dua Lipa represents coffee as a lifestyle choice: aesthetic, customizable, social-media native. And when she uses his "What else?" line in the ad, Clooney reacts, half offended, half amused. It is a small moment, but it says something important. The line belongs to the brand now, not just to him. By passing it between generations, Nespresso made that clear without stating it outright.

Then there is The Weeknd.

His collaboration with Nespresso, titled "A Tribute to Origins," takes a completely different direction. It honors his Ethiopian roots and pays tribute to his mother Samra, framing coffee not as a luxury ritual but as a story of heritage. Every cup tells a story. It is quiet, personal, and emotionally specific in a way that neither Clooney nor Dua Lipa's campaigns are.

And that is the point.

Each ambassador is doing a different job. Clooney signals premium and wit. Dua Lipa signals youth and modernity. The Weeknd signals depth and origin. Together, they let Nespresso speak to different people and different values without contradicting itself.

Relevance does not require reinvention. Sometimes it just requires knowing what to keep.

The lesson is not just "use celebrities." It is know what you want each one to say, and make sure they are saying something different. Relevance does not require reinvention. Sometimes it just requires knowing what to keep.
