A Very Earnest
Marketing Paper

Consumer Research A/B Testing Advertising Consumer Psychology

my take

The assignment: pick a real Vancouver business, diagnose what's broken in their marketing, build two campaigns to fix it, then test whether they actually worked.

We chose Earnest Ice Cream. The problem wasn't the product, it was the gap between how people experience Earnest in person and how the brand shows up online. Warm, social, a Friday evening thing. Online: product shots and ingredient lists. The feeling wasn't there.

We built two ads to close that gap, each targeting a different segment. One leaned into post-work reward culture for young professionals. The other leaned into family nostalgia for millennial parents. Then we ran an A/B experiment on 200 simulated consumers to see which moved purchase likelihood more.

The hypothesis made sense on paper: match the ad to the segment. What actually happened: the nostalgia-forward family ad outperformed across both groups. Gen Z responded to it too.

Which tells you something true about ice cream and hedonic products generally. People don't buy a scoop because it fits their demographic. They buy it because it connects to something warm. Nostalgia doesn't have a target audience.

Academic project · UBC Sauder School of Business. Not affiliated with or commissioned by Earnest Ice Cream.

from the project

Storyboards for both strategies. Left: "My 5-9 after 9-5" targeting Gen Z young professionals. Right: "A Scoop of Joy" targeting millennial parents. Same brand, two completely different emotional registers.

key finding

The family-oriented ad produced a statistically significant difference in purchase likelihood between segments (p = 0.04). The Gen Z-targeted ad did not (p = 0.78). Emotional warmth moved both groups. Segment-specific messaging only moved one. When the product is inherently hedonic, lean into the feeling, not the demographic.
