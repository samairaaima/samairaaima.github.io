Fenty Beauty:
Brand Audit

Brand Audit Primary Research Brand Extensions Data Analysis

my take

The assignment: a full brand audit of Fenty Beauty, with a focus on its extension into Savage x Fenty lingerie.

We went into Sephora. Multiple locations, in person, asking real shoppers what came to mind when they thought of Fenty: unprompted first, then with a small nudge, then structured questions about purchase behaviour and spending. Fifty-two respondents total. The in-person piece mattered because you catch things a Qualtrics survey doesn't.

The course question was whether brand extensions work: whether meaning transfers from one category to another. In Fenty's case, the answer was: mostly yes, but not in the way they probably intended. Rihanna transfers. Inclusivity transfers. Savage x Fenty barely showed up in consumer associations at all.

My heaviest contribution was the variance analysis: figuring out what actually predicts how much someone spends on Fenty. Celebrity influence, activism, pop culture interest: none of it correlated meaningfully with spending. What did: performance-based associations. Loyal buyers think about the product. Casual fans think about Rihanna.

A brand can be culturally iconic and functionally underperforming at the same time. Fenty is both.

Primary and secondary association data from 52 in-person respondents. Rihanna leads at 19.5% of primary associations. Savage x Fenty barely registers. The extension succeeded commercially but didn't move into consumer memory the way the parent brand did.

key finding

High-SOP customers averaged fewer associations than low-SOP customers, but those associations were more positive and more performance-focused. The people who spend the most on Fenty think about the product, not the founder. Brand loyalty lives in the product experience, not the cultural moment.
